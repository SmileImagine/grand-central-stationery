# Grand Central Stationery

A classroom display page for music lessons: typed text and typed music shown at the largest size that fits the screen, with note names colour-coded (ChromaNotes). One HTML file, no build step, and everything needed to run offline is in this folder.

**Live:** https://smileimagine.github.io/grand-central-stationery/ (the ⋯ menu links to the practice board and the Rhythm & Dance lab)

Nothing is uploaded: lessons are saved in the browser you use, and a Share link carries a lesson inside the address itself.

## Running

The page opens directly from Finder, playback included. `serve.command` starts a local server (`serve.js` with Node, else Python) and opens the page; the tune editor can save only through `serve.js`; it loads slightly faster because the served page fetches the 88 piano samples individually, while a page opened from disk loads them all from one embedded file.

## Using it

**Cells.** `+ Text`, `+ Notation`, `+ Rhythm` and `+ Dance` add cells; Cmd+Enter inside a cell adds a text cell below it (Cmd+Shift+Enter a notation cell). Reorder by dragging the grip (⠿) or by typing a position in the number box. ✕ deletes at once; Undo in the toast (or Cmd+Z outside a text field) brings the cell back. Cmd+Z and Shift+Cmd+Z inside a cell undo and redo its text.

**Text.** Type; note names A–G colour themselves, and `#` or `b` after a note becomes ♯ or ♭ at no extra width. Newlines are respected. Each text cell has its own font (several legible faces; a new cell starts in the one chosen last) and a Centred box that centres the text across and down the cell, for a title.

**Notation.** Type ABC, e.g. `C D E F | G A B c |` with `w: do re mi fa` for lyrics. `L:1/4` is the default length and 80 BPM the default tempo, `C2` doubles, `z` rests, `[CEG]` chords, `K:G` for a key, `Q:1/4=90` for a tempo. Noteheads carry their letter and colour; accidentals sit small beside the head. Play gives a two-beat count-in, a clock hand that sweeps through each measure, a warm beam over the current bar, a cursor in the colour of each note, and a lamp that pulses on the beat. The page plays every sound itself, on one AudioContext clock: abcjs supplies the parsed tune, the engraving and the note timings, and a scheduler of the page's own plays the melody, the accompaniment and the Band from them, so the picture, the melody and the band cannot drift apart, a tempo change keeps its place, and the repeat button loops without a gap (switched off mid-tune, the tune finishes its time round). Drag the cursor along the staff to seek, before or during playback. The tempo figure under the title is directly editable in beats per minute; Transpose shifts letters, colours and sound together. The Band's arrangement can live in the text: lines beginning `%%band` describe each time round (`%%band 1: energy=30 | G | D7 | Em | C |`, `%%band 2: energy=70 detail=90 | G Bm7 | Am7 D7 | ...`), with one bar per bar of the tune, the chords in a bar sharing it equally, `.` carrying a chord on and `-` leaving a bar to the drums; `kick=`, `snare=`, `hat=` and `comp=` take step strings (`x` hit, `o` soft, `.` rest). With Repeat on, the choruses take turns, the chord symbols relabel themselves each time round, and the instrument follows the chorus's chords. Arrange writes five such lines from the melody and its chords (plain, sevenths, approach chords checked against the melody, full energy, a quiet last time) to keep or edit. Feel plays the melody as a musician would rather than as written: slurs `( )` mark the phrases when the tune writes them (otherwise rests, long notes at bar lines and four-bar spans do); phrases rise to their high point and settle, the beat has weight and the off-beat less, steps join and repeated notes separate, the last note of a phrase lingers and the next phrase comes after a breath, and each instrument adds its own touch (the recorder scoops into a phrase and warms a long note with vibrato, the ukulele hammers on into a leap and strums its chords, the piano pedals); at 0 the text is played exactly. Under the staff (and the instrument) runs one bar: the transport, the tempo and three tabs, Melody, Band and Mixer, one drawer open at a time. Melody holds the tune's ABC beside the instrument's controls (Instrument, Names, Chords, Transpose, Feel); its ABC hints work like a code editor's, red wavy underlines in the text box for what abcjs would leave silent and grey for tips; pointing at an underline, or putting the caret in it with a click or the arrow keys, opens its hint in a small card just under it with ? for its explanation and × to dismiss it, a count in the foot of the box opens every hint in a list (a hint there takes the caret to its place; the dismissed ones can be shown again), and a red tab count marks items that need attention; Band holds the arrangement as text (the `%%band` lines, shown without their prefix, so `1: energy=30 | G | D7 | Em | C |` and `drums: bd ~ sd ~`) beside the band's controls and a note saying what the band will play; Mixer shows the band's members (melody, chords, bass, drums), a level slider for each and a click on the figure to mute them, and the levels are saved with the cell. Editing, the Melody drawer is open and the mixer closes when the music starts; presenting and in the game, the bar stands alone until a tab is tapped and every drawer closes when the music starts; the Melody drawer shows the ABC in the game as it does elsewhere. The two boxes are one text in the document (the melody, then its `%%band` lines), so a lesson exported or shared is unchanged. Instrument also chooses the playback sound (piano, electric piano, nylon guitar, recorder) and shows a piano, a Wicki-Hayden keyboard (ISO QWERTY rows with J as middle C; Transpose moves the pitches on the keys so J stays the root and the fingering does not change), a ukulele in the player's perspective (the near G string larger and closest, the A string farthest, with the frets, body, headstock and machine heads sharing the same slight projection) whose four-string tablature can replace the staff in the score, or a large recorder (the sounding fingering solid; holes that must change for the next note fade in with its colour and flicker) under the staff that plays along (the choice is remembered, and a new notation cell starts with it): the sounding note's key is lit, the next note's key is marked on deck, and a runner leaves the sounding key as it plays and arrives on the next as it sounds, so its speed shows how far the hand must move in the time it has; an open-string transition fades out and back at the nut instead of pretending the fretting hand travelled there. The ukulele is drawn from the player's side in a mild perspective, the G string nearest and the A string farthest: the far edge is a little shorter than the near one so the board's ends lean, about twelve degrees at the nut and five at the bridge, the far rows crowd a little, and marks on far strings come out a little smaller; the body and headstock are not drawn as objects but suggested by wood grain that fades into the page, with one ring for the soundhole and a bar for the bridge, so a child can see which way the diagram sits on the instrument in their hands and that strumming happens between the soundhole and the bridge. The two hands are shown apart: a plucked string glows in its note's colour from nut to bridge and its own plectrum over the soundhole lights with it, open or fretted, the glow rising through the note before so that it peaks at the onset and fades after; a circle sits on the fret only for a fretted note, and the runner, which is the fretting hand, rests where it was over an open string rather than travelling to the nut. The ukulele picks the place nearest the hand's last fret; fixed-range instruments shift the tune by octaves to fit. Proportional (toolbar) spaces notes by duration so the cursor sweeps evenly. The hints never change the cell's size: the card and the list float over the page. Items to check point out what abcjs leaves silent: a bar that is longer or shorter than the metre (or than 4/4 when there is no M: line), a tie between different notes (abcjs swallows the next note of that pitch; a slur wants parentheses), a lyric line whose syllables do not match its notes, a transposition that lands in a key with five or more sharps or flats (with a friendlier interval nearby), notes the chosen instrument cannot show, and abcjs's own parse errors reworded with line and character. Muted tips note things worth knowing: no K: line, a broken rhythm (C>D), a leap of more than an octave, an accidental repeated within a bar, lyrics over rests, and an instrument view shifted by octaves. The ? beside each opens an explanation with a corrected example, and × dismisses that item for the editing session. A tune with no L: line gets L:1/4 whether or not it has a K: line.

**Rhythm.** One line per drum, `name: x... x.x.`: `x` a hit, `X` an accent, `o` soft, `.` a rest, `|` a bar; the steps of a bar share it equally, so a line of 3 against a line of 2 is a polyrhythm. `meter: 3/4`, `bpm: 100`. Drums: kick snare hat open rim clap tom low wood bell clave shaker crash ride; body: stomp clap pat snap click. Views: Grid (box notation), Wheel (one ring per drum), Syllables (stick notation with the words under each onset: Kodály, fruit words, 1 e & a counting, takadimi or Carnatic konnakol) and Body (the pattern as body percussion on a figure). A bar of count-in, a click, repeat.

**Dance.** One token per beat on a `feet:` line: `L`/`R` step onto a foot, `l`/`r` touch it down, with a direction `f b s x z c` (forward, back, side, across, across behind, close), `J` jump, `H` hop, `K` kick, `.` hold, `Ls-Rc` two moves in a beat; `arms: u o d c h`; `level: l m h`, `lean: f b l r c`, `hips: l r f b c`, `shoulders: l r u c`, `turn: r` or `l` per quarter turn; effort marks after a token (`!` sudden `~` sustained `_` strong `^` light `@` indirect `=` bound) shape how it moves. Drum lines in the same cell play underneath; without them a click keeps time. Views: Dancer (a rigged figure: the feet are a contact timeline, the pelvis follows the supporting foot, knees and elbows bend to reach), Footprints, Figures, Columns (Labanotation-style) and Stave (Benesh-style). Seen from shows the dancer from behind, from in front (mirrored, for a class copying the teacher) or from three-quarters.

**Playing along.** The loop button in the transport repeats the tune without a gap. Play chooses what sounds: the melody with its chord symbols, the chords alone as a backing track while the picture still follows the melody, or the melody alone. Chords is Auto by default: the written chord symbols sound, and a tune with none gets a chord for each half bar found from the melody and the key, shown and played (and a tune whose written chords stop short, such as a round with symbols only in its first bar, gets found chords before the first symbol and after the last), so every tune has a backing track without a visit to the Band tab; Numerals adds a Roman numeral under each chord symbol, coloured by function (green tonic, blue subdominant, red dominant); Written only leaves a tune without chords alone. Written chord symbols always win, and so does an arrangement in the Band tab. Style chooses the groove, and every style is played by the same band, the page's own accompanist: chord voicings on electric piano that move by the smallest step from one chord to the next, a bass line of roots and fifths with approach notes into the next chord, and a drum kit whose patterns thicken with the Energy slider, vary from bar to bar, and fill at the end of every fourth bar; Detail adds sevenths, ninths, approach notes and ghost notes, and the arrow deals a new variation. With Repeat on and nothing in the arrangement box, each time round builds on the last without a button: the tune as written, then sevenths with a little more energy, then approach chords into each change, then the fullest, then a quieter last time with a dominant into the final bar; Arrange writes that same arrangement into the box to keep or edit. Auto, the default, reads the tune's metre and its `R:` line: a jig in 6/8, a waltz in 3/4, swing for swing, jazz, blues and rags, bossa for Latin rhythms, a march for marches and polkas, a ballad for airs and lullabies, boom-chick for reels, hornpipes and tunes in 2/4, and pop and rock otherwise. A tune can name its own default with a `%%style` line in the ABC (`%%style drone`), which Auto takes before the metre and the `R:` line, so a transcription carries the accompaniment that suits it. The styles can also be chosen by name: Pop and rock, Folk (boom-chick), March and polka, Waltz, Jig, Swing (the band's and the melody's off-beat eighths land late), Bossa (rim clave, anticipated bass), Ballad (arpeggios over a pad), and Drone (a tanpura's held root and fifth with a tabla-like pattern on the low drum and the rim; the harmony stays on the tonic whatever the melody suggests, as a raga's does). A style is a groove the sliders write in the band's own pattern language, so the Band box shows it in grey and it can be edited there; a style that does not fit the metre gives way (a waltz on a 4/4 tune plays boom-chick). At low Energy a soft pad holds each chord under the sparse hits, and the drummer clicks the count-in. The band is scheduled from the exact AudioContext time at which abcjs starts its own audio, so it stays locked to the melody. A page opened from disk has no samples for the band, so there abcjs's own patterns play the chords, without drums.

**Tuning.** A tune can be played in a scale of its own. Tuning, in the Melody tab, takes a Scala `.scl` file pasted in (the kind a microtonal keyboard exports) and writes it into the ABC as one line, `%%tuning slendro: 231.0 474.0 717.0 955.0 2/1`: the degrees above 1/1 in cents (a number with a point) or as ratios, the last one the octave. Every pitched note, the melody's and the band's, is then detuned to its degree while the staff shows the written notes. The root is the key's tonic unless `root=` names a note. `map=` says which written notes take which degrees: `semitones` puts one degree on each semitone from the root (the default for twelve-note scales), `degrees` puts the scale's steps on the key's own steps, do re mi so la for five and the diatonic scale for seven (the default for those), and `nearest` gives each written note the degree nearest it in cents (the default otherwise, as for a 22-shruti scale). A tip under the text names the tuning in force; abcjs sees the line as a comment. With a tuning in force the Wicki-Hayden keyboard lays the scale out as the joyboard does: the J key is the root (1/1), each key to the right is the degree nearest a whole tone up (2/12 of the scale), each row up the degree nearest a fifth less that, nudged until the two axes reach every degree, so a five-note scale steps by one degree and a 22-sruti scale by four; keys are labelled by degree, 1 at the root, and the runner and the lit keys follow the tuned pitches. When an ordinary Wicki-Hayden pitch occurs on more than one key, the whole phrase is assigned to the duplicate-key path with the least hand travel. Piano and ukulele remain useful 12-tone landmarks and say explicitly that their keys or fixed frets are approximations while playback stays tuned. The recorder keeps its ordinary fingering diagram but adds each tuned pitch's displacement in cents: an upward cue suggests firmer air or a slight leak, a downward cue gentler air or shading; exact response and alternate fingerings depend on the recorder and the note.

**The band's score as text.** The Band tab has one text box for the band. What is typed there is the arrangement; under it, in grey, are the lines the sliders write for every part not typed (`drums:`, `bass:`, `chords:`), so the band's parts can be read where they are edited. The parts are written in a small pattern language of the TidalCycles and Strudel family written for this page (so the page stays one file under one licence). One bar is one cycle, and the steps of a line share the bar equally, so `bd ~ sd ~` is a kick on one and a snare on three. `~` rests, `_` holds the step before it on, `[ ]` subdivides a step, `< >` alternates bar by bar, `*` and `/` speed a step up or slow it down, `!` repeats it, `@` weights it, `?` drops it by chance (`?0.3` for a 30% chance), `(3,8)` spreads three hits over eight steps the Euclidean way, `:` sets a level (`sd:.2` is a ghost note), `,` and a new line stack parts, and `//` starts a comment. Each line names its part: `drums:`, `bass:` or `chords:`, and a part's lines stack. Drum words are `bd` kick, `sd` snare, `hh` closed hat, `oh` open hat, `rim`, `cp` clap, `cr` crash, `rd` ride, `sh` shaker and `tb` tambourine. Bass words are relative to the chord in force: `r` the root, `3` `5` `7` its third, fifth and seventh, `8` the root an octave up, `a` an approach note into the next chord, `n` the next chord's root. Chord words are `ch` the voicing (voice-led as before), `pad` the voicing held softly, and `c1`..`c4` its notes from the bottom, for arpeggios. Control lines automate the band over bars: `energy =`, `detail =`, `drums =`, `bass =` and `chords =` take a pattern of numbers from 0 to 1 (`energy = <.3 .5 .7 .9>` steps up over four bars) or of signals (`sine`, `tri`, `saw`, `square`, `rand`, with `:lo:hi` for a range), read at each hit, so `energy = sine/8:.3:.9` is an LFO that swells and fades over eight bars; the part names set a level. While the sliders write the grey lines, moving them rewrites them, which is the lesson: a band's part is a few lines a class can read. Press Play with the Band tab open: each word lights as it sounds, and the foot of the box says what it became on the chord in force (`bass a → F♯ (D7 → G)`, `chords ch → G: G B D`), so the two halves of the accompaniment are seen together, the arrangement's chords choosing the notes and the pattern choosing when. Edit a grey line and the pattern is yours: the lines become typed text and go into the ABC as `%%band` lines (the grey lines themselves are never saved) (`%%band bass: r _ 5 ~`, `%%band energy = sine/8:.3:.9`; a number before the colon or the equals sign, as in `%%band drums 2:`, gives the second time round its own), a part you leave out is still the sliders', and a mistake is named in the foot of the box and in the band note while that part plays the sliders' version. Back to the sliders, in the foot of the box, takes the typed pattern lines out. The chart's `kick=`, `snare=`, `hat=` and `comp=` step strings are read as the same language.

**Sharing a tune and its band.** Copy as Strudel, in the Band tab, copies the whole thing as text to send: the ABC of the tune, then the band as it plays (typed or the sliders') as Strudel-like lines, `$: s("bd ~ sd ~, hh*8")` for the drums, `$: note("r _ 5 _").s("bass")` and `$: note("ch _ _ _").s("piano")` for the bass and the chords, and the control lines as they are. Paste… opens a box to paste into, and the paste is read in at once (or with Use): a tune in ABC, Strudel code, the score's own `drums:` lines, or any of them together. Strudel code is read down to its pattern strings and a few methods (`.fast` and `.slow` become `*` and `/`, `.gain` a level, `.degradeBy` a chance, `.euclid` and `.struct` a rhythm, `stack()` its lines; `bd:3` picks a sample in Strudel and is just `bd` here, `bd . sd sd` groups as it does there); `.room(0..1)` becomes the cell's shared reverb send (`room =` in the pattern score), while effects such as `.lpf()` are left out quietly, methods the page does not know are named and left out, and a pattern whose words are not the page's (a `note("c e g")` melody, say) is named and skipped while the rest goes in. The band lines go into the Band box (where they are `%%band` lines in the saved ABC), so what is shared reads as patterns, and a tune pasted without band lines arrives as it is. The page's language is its own, so lines written here will not all run in Strudel, and Strudel's `{ }` polymeter, `%` and `.every()` do not run here.

**Game.** The Game button in a notation cell's header fills the screen with the current and next systems under the cursor (or the former one-line scrolling view when Lines is set to One), the instrument view large (it keeps at least a quarter of the screen whatever drawer is open, and the drawers close when the music starts), a next-up departure sign, progress bar, count-in digits and a flourish at the end; Esc returns. Names switches the staff, instrument and runner between letters and movable-do solfège (la-based minor). Chord symbols in the ABC (`"G"`, `"D7"`, `"Em"`) get a small Wicki-Hayden shape beside the name and outline their keys on the piano and Wicki-Hayden views while they are in force.

**Presenting.** Present shows one cell at a time: PageDown / PageUp move, the number box jumps, Space plays and pauses a notation, rhythm or dance cell, Esc returns. Present 3D lays the cells out in space (impress.js), starting from an overview. Set up (beside the slide number), or the 3D button in a cell's header, opens sliders for one cell at a time: right, down and nearer place it from the cell before, in screens, so the cells after it move along like a path; roll, pitch and yaw turn it, size scales it, and colour sets the stage behind it. A slider shows the whole layout with the cell outlined and moving as you drag; a number box takes an exact value, and a blank box (or a double-click on the slider) is automatic. Click a cell on the overview to choose it, Fly in plays the flight from the cell before, Auto returns the cell to the automatic layout, and Reset 3D layout on the overview clears every cell (with Undo). Plain Present is the lighter mode: no 3D transforms, so text stays crisp and it works on any machine.

**Menu (⋯).** Proportional spacing, Share link, Export, Import and Clear.

**Sharing.** Share link copies a URL that carries every cell, compressed, in the address itself (no server involved). Export writes the same content as a text file; Import reads one back.

### Document format

Plain text. An optional first line `=== page {json}` holds page settings (proportional spacing; an older file's `font` is given to its text cells). Then, per cell, a separator line `=== text`, `=== abc`, `=== rhythm` or `=== dance` (an older `=== heading` opens as a centred text cell), optionally followed by json (`dx dy dz` for the 3D place, in screens from the cell before, and `r rx ry s` for its roll, pitch, yaw and scale; an older file's `x y z` in pixels is rewritten on opening; `bg` background, `font` and `align` for a text cell, `tr` transposition, `inst` instrument view, `mv` the rhythm or dance view, `fac` the dance's viewpoint), and the cell's text verbatim until the next separator. A file without separators becomes one text cell.

## On a phone or tablet

The page adapts below 700 px: the notation transport moves under the ABC text, cell tools are always visible on touch screens, and in Present mode the navigation sits at the top corner. The playback cursor's drag handle is wider for fingers.

## Sample cells

A first visit (or an empty page after Clear) shows four sample cells. They live in the `SAMPLE` list near the top of the *Cells* section of `index.html`, one entry per cell with a `type` (`text` or `abc`), its `text`, and for text optionally `align: 'center'` or a `font`; edit that list to change them. Once a lesson has been saved in a browser, the samples are not shown again there.

## Offline assets

- `vendor/abcjs-basic-min.js`: abcjs 6.7.0 (MIT), used for parsing, engraving and note timing; the page plays the sound itself.
- `vendor/impress.js`: impress.js 2.0.0 (MIT), used by Present 3D.
- `vendor/soundfont/`: samples from midi-js-soundfonts (MIT, the FluidR3_GM set throughout, so every instrument plays at the same level), used by abcjs playback, plus the acoustic bass and the percussion notes the accompaniment uses: piano, and the recorder, nylon guitar (for the ukulele) and electric piano (for the Wicki-Hayden keyboard) that the Instrument views play with; `piano-embedded.js` holds the piano as base64 for pages opened from disk, which therefore play every view on the piano.
- `fonts/`: local copies of Atkinson Hyperlegible, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono, Lexend, Andika and Noto Music (all SIL Open Font License), with `fonts/fonts.css`.
- The wordmark is inline SVG outlines made from Cinzel and Caveat (both SIL Open Font License), so it needs no font request.
- `favicon.svg`, `favicon-32.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`: the arch-and-staff mark (the SVG follows the system's light or dark mode).
- `gcs-accidentals.woff2`: two zero-advance glyphs (♯ ♭) derived from Noto Music so "C♭" is as wide as "C"; also embedded in the pages.
