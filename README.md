# Grand Central Stationery

A classroom display page for music lessons: typed text and typed music shown at the largest size that fits the screen, with note names colour-coded (ChromaNotes). One HTML file, no build step, and everything needed to run offline is in this folder.

**Live:** https://smileimagine.github.io/grand-central-stationery/ (the ⋯ menu links to the practice board and the Rhythm & Dance lab)

Nothing is uploaded: lessons are saved in the browser you use, and a Share link carries a lesson inside the address itself.

## Layout

| `course/` | Tune grading and sequencing for a practice course: `analyze.js` measures an ABC tune, tags the skills it needs and grades it on a skill lattice; `analyze-cli.js` runs it over a folder of tunes and writes a suggested sequence and a tune set; `board.html` is the practice board: students pick their name, see their level, choose tunes to practise and flag them ready; the teacher marks passes, and can keep the board to the tunes for an Ontario grade. See `course/README.md`. |
| `lab/` | Single-cell test pages the main page grew out of: `Notation v1.html` (abcjs), `Text Proportional v1.html` (canvas fitter, font picker), `Text Mono Grid v4.html` (monospace grid fitter); and `Rhythm and Dance v1.html`, prototypes for teaching pure rhythm and dance steps from text, with no libraries and synthesised sounds: four rhythm views (grid, wheel, Kodály syllables with stick notation, body percussion) and four dance views (footprints, Labanotation-style columns, Benesh-style stave, stick-figure strip), all painted from one clock; a picker loads the course's graded rhythm and dance sets when the page is served. |

## Running

The page opens directly from Finder, playback included. `serve.command` starts a local server and opens the page; it loads slightly faster because the served page fetches the 88 piano samples individually, while a page opened from disk loads them all from one embedded file.

## Using it

**Cells.** `+ Text`, `+ Heading`, `+ Notation`, `+ Rhythm` and `+ Dance` add cells; Cmd+Enter inside a cell adds a text cell below it (Cmd+Shift+Enter a notation cell). Reorder by dragging the grip (⠿) or by typing a position in the number box. ✕ deletes at once; Undo in the toast (or Cmd+Z outside a text field) brings the cell back. Cmd+Z and Shift+Cmd+Z inside a cell undo and redo its text.

**Text.** Type; note names A–G colour themselves, and `#` or `b` after a note becomes ♯ or ♭ at no extra width. Newlines are respected. The font menu offers several legible faces.

**Notation.** Type ABC, e.g. `C D E F | G A B c |` with `w: do re mi fa` for lyrics. `L:1/4` is the default length and 80 BPM the default tempo, `C2` doubles, `z` rests, `[CEG]` chords, `K:G` for a key, `Q:1/4=90` for a tempo. Noteheads carry their letter and colour; accidentals sit small beside the head. Play gives a two-beat count-in, a cursor in the colour of each note, and a lamp that pulses on the beat. The page plays every sound itself, on one AudioContext clock: abcjs supplies the parsed tune, the engraving and the note timings, and a scheduler of the page's own plays the melody, the accompaniment and the Band from them, so the picture, the melody and the band cannot drift apart, a tempo change keeps its place, and the repeat button loops without a gap (switched off mid-tune, the tune finishes its time round). Drag the cursor along the staff to seek, before or during playback. Tempo sets the playback speed in beats per minute; Transpose shifts letters, colours and sound together. The Band's arrangement can live in the text: lines beginning `%%band` describe each time round (`%%band 1: energy=30 | G | D7 | Em | C |`, `%%band 2: energy=70 detail=90 | G Bm7 | Am7 D7 | ...`), with one bar per bar of the tune, the chords in a bar sharing it equally, `.` carrying a chord on and `-` leaving a bar to the drums; `kick=`, `snare=`, `hat=` and `comp=` take step strings (`x` hit, `o` soft, `.` rest). With Repeat on, the choruses take turns, the chord symbols relabel themselves each time round, and the instrument follows the chorus's chords. Arrange writes five such lines from the melody and its chords (plain, sevenths, approach chords checked against the melody, full energy, a quiet last time) to keep or edit. Feel plays the melody as a musician would rather than as written: slurs `( )` mark the phrases when the tune writes them (otherwise rests, long notes at bar lines and four-bar spans do); phrases rise to their high point and settle, the beat has weight and the off-beat less, steps join and repeated notes separate, the last note of a phrase lingers and the next phrase comes after a breath, and each instrument adds its own touch (the recorder scoops into a phrase and warms a long note with vibrato, the ukulele hammers on into a leap and strums its chords, the piano pedals); at 0 the text is played exactly. Under the staff (and the instrument) runs one bar: the transport, the tempo and three tabs, Melody, Band and Mixer, one drawer open at a time. Melody holds the tune's ABC beside the instrument's controls (Instrument, Names, Chords, Transpose, Feel), with a red count on the tab for the warnings under the text; Band holds the arrangement as text (the `%%band` lines, shown without their prefix, so `1: energy=30 | G | D7 | Em | C |` and `drums: bd ~ sd ~`) beside the band's controls and a note saying what the band will play; Mixer shows the band's members (melody, chords, bass, drums), a level slider for each and a click on the figure to mute them, and the levels are saved with the cell. Editing, the Melody drawer is open and the mixer closes when the music starts; presenting and in the game, the bar stands alone until a tab is tapped and every drawer closes when the music starts. The two boxes are one text in the document (the melody, then its `%%band` lines), so a lesson exported or shared is unchanged. Instrument also chooses the playback sound (piano, electric piano, nylon guitar, recorder) and shows a piano, a Wicki-Hayden keyboard (ISO QWERTY rows with J as middle C; Transpose moves the pitches on the keys so J stays the root and the fingering does not change), a ukulele (drawn as it lies on the player's lap, face up, neck to the left: headstock and pegs past the nut, frets closing up toward the body as a real fretboard's do, the body's bouts with the soundhole and bridge at the right, and a caption saying so) with a scrolling tab lane, or a large recorder (the sounding fingering solid; holes that must change for the next note fade in with its colour and flicker) under the staff that plays along (the choice is remembered, and a new notation cell starts with it): the sounding note's key is lit, the next note's key is marked on deck, and a runner leaves the sounding key as it plays and arrives on the next as it sounds, so its speed shows how far the hand must move in the time it has. The ukulele picks the place nearest the hand's last fret; fixed-range instruments shift the tune by octaves to fit. Proportional (toolbar) spaces notes by duration so the cursor sweeps evenly. Under the transport, warnings (red) point out what abcjs leaves silent: a bar that is longer or shorter than the metre (or than 4/4 when there is no M: line), a tie between different notes (abcjs swallows the next note of that pitch; a slur wants parentheses), a lyric line whose syllables do not match its notes, a transposition that lands in a key with five or more sharps or flats (with a friendlier interval nearby), notes the chosen instrument cannot show, and abcjs's own parse errors reworded with line and character. Tips (grey) note things worth knowing: no K: line, a broken rhythm (C>D), a leap of more than an octave, an accidental repeated within a bar, lyrics over rests, and an instrument view shifted by octaves. The ? beside each opens an explanation with a corrected example. A tune with no L: line gets L:1/4 whether or not it has a K: line.

**Rhythm.** One line per drum, `name: x... x.x.`: `x` a hit, `X` an accent, `o` soft, `.` a rest, `|` a bar; the steps of a bar share it equally, so a line of 3 against a line of 2 is a polyrhythm. `meter: 3/4`, `bpm: 100`. Drums: kick snare hat open rim clap tom low wood bell clave shaker crash ride; body: stomp clap pat snap click. Views: Grid (box notation), Wheel (one ring per drum), Syllables (stick notation with the words under each onset: Kodály, fruit words, 1 e & a counting, takadimi or Carnatic konnakol) and Body (the pattern as body percussion on a figure). A bar of count-in, a click, repeat.

**Dance.** One token per beat on a `feet:` line: `L`/`R` step onto a foot, `l`/`r` touch it down, with a direction `f b s x z c` (forward, back, side, across, across behind, close), `J` jump, `H` hop, `K` kick, `.` hold, `Ls-Rc` two moves in a beat; `arms: u o d c h`; `level: l m h`, `lean: f b l r c`, `hips: l r f b c`, `shoulders: l r u c`, `turn: r` or `l` per quarter turn; effort marks after a token (`!` sudden `~` sustained `_` strong `^` light `@` indirect `=` bound) shape how it moves. Drum lines in the same cell play underneath; without them a click keeps time. Views: Dancer (a rigged figure: the feet are a contact timeline, the pelvis follows the supporting foot, knees and elbows bend to reach), Footprints, Figures, Columns (Labanotation-style) and Stave (Benesh-style). Seen from shows the dancer from behind, from in front (mirrored, for a class copying the teacher) or from three-quarters.

**The band's score as text.** The Pattern button in the Band tab opens the band's parts as text, in a small pattern language of the TidalCycles and Strudel family written for this page (so the page stays one file under one licence). One bar is one cycle, and the steps of a line share the bar equally, so `bd ~ sd ~` is a kick on one and a snare on three. `~` rests, `_` holds the step before it on, `[ ]` subdivides a step, `< >` alternates bar by bar, `*` and `/` speed a step up or slow it down, `!` repeats it, `@` weights it, `?` drops it by chance (`?0.3` for a 30% chance), `(3,8)` spreads three hits over eight steps the Euclidean way, `:` sets a level (`sd:.2` is a ghost note), `,` and a new line stack parts, and `//` starts a comment. The score has sections: `drums:`, `bass:` and `chords:` open a part (lines before any label are drums). Drum words are `bd` kick, `sd` snare, `hh` closed hat, `oh` open hat, `rim`, `cp` clap, `cr` crash, `rd` ride, `sh` shaker and `tb` tambourine. Bass words are relative to the chord in force: `r` the root, `3` `5` `7` its third, fifth and seventh, `8` the root an octave up, `a` an approach note into the next chord, `n` the next chord's root. Chord words are `ch` the voicing (voice-led as before), `pad` the voicing held softly, and `c1`..`c4` its notes from the bottom, for arpeggios. Control lines automate the band over bars: `energy =`, `detail =`, `drums =`, `bass =` and `chords =` take a pattern of numbers from 0 to 1 (`energy = <.3 .5 .7 .9>` steps up over four bars) or of signals (`sine`, `tri`, `saw`, `square`, `rand`, with `:lo:hi` for a range), read at each hit, so `energy = sine/8:.3:.9` is an LFO that swells and fades over eight bars; the part names set a level. While the sliders write the text, moving them rewrites it, which is the lesson: a band's part is a few lines a class can read. Edit the text and it is yours: it goes into the ABC as `%%band` lines (`%%band bass: r _ 5 ~`, `%%band energy = sine/8:.3:.9`; a number before the colon or the equals sign, as in `%%band drums 2:`, gives the second time round its own), a part you leave out is still the sliders', and a mistake is named under the box and in the warnings while that part plays the sliders' version. Back to the sliders takes the score lines out. The chart's `kick=`, `snare=`, `hat=` and `comp=` step strings are read as the same language.

**Game.** The Game button in a notation cell's header fills the screen with that tune on one scrolling line under a fixed cursor, the instrument view large (it keeps at least a quarter of the screen whatever drawer is open, and the drawers close when the music starts), a progress bar, count-in digits and a flourish at the end; Esc returns. Names switches the staff, instrument and runner between letters and movable-do solfège (la-based minor). Chord symbols in the ABC (`"G"`, `"D7"`, `"Em"`) get a small Wicki-Hayden shape beside the name and outline their keys on the piano and Wicki-Hayden views while they are in force.

**Presenting.** Present shows one cell at a time: PageDown / PageUp move, the number box jumps, Space plays and pauses a notation, rhythm or dance cell, Esc returns. Present 3D lays the cells out in space (impress.js), starting from an overview; the 3D button in a cell's header sets its position, rotation, scale and stage background, and Reset 3D layout on the overview clears them all (with Undo). Plain Present is the lighter mode: no 3D transforms, so text stays crisp and it works on any machine.

**Menu (⋯).** Text font, proportional spacing, Share link, Export, Import and Clear.

**Sharing.** Share link copies a URL that carries every cell, compressed, in the address itself (no server involved). Export writes the same content as a text file; Import reads one back.

### Document format

Plain text. An optional first line `=== page {json}` holds page settings (text font, proportional spacing). Then, per cell, a separator line `=== heading`, `=== text`, `=== abc`, `=== rhythm` or `=== dance`, optionally followed by json (`x y z r rx ry s` for 3D position, `bg` background, `tr` transposition, `inst` instrument view, `mv` the rhythm or dance view, `fac` the dance's viewpoint), and the cell's text verbatim until the next separator. A file without separators becomes one text cell.

## On a phone or tablet

The page adapts below 700 px: the notation transport moves under the ABC text, cell tools are always visible on touch screens, and in Present mode the navigation sits at the top corner. The playback cursor's drag handle is wider for fingers.

## Sample cells

A first visit (or an empty page after Clear) shows four sample cells. They live in the `SAMPLE` list near the top of the *Cells* section of `index.html`, one entry per cell with a `type` (`heading`, `text` or `abc`) and its `text`; edit that list to change them. Once a lesson has been saved in a browser, the samples are not shown again there.

## Offline assets

- `vendor/abcjs-basic-min.js`: abcjs 6.7.0 (MIT), used for parsing, engraving and note timing; the page plays the sound itself.
- `vendor/impress.js`: impress.js 2.0.0 (MIT), used by Present 3D.
- `vendor/soundfont/`: samples from midi-js-soundfonts (MIT, the FluidR3_GM set throughout, so every instrument plays at the same level), used by abcjs playback, plus the acoustic bass and the percussion notes the accompaniment uses: piano, and the recorder, nylon guitar (for the ukulele) and electric piano (for the Wicki-Hayden keyboard) that the Instrument views play with; `piano-embedded.js` holds the piano as base64 for pages opened from disk, which therefore play every view on the piano.
- `fonts/`: local copies of Atkinson Hyperlegible, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono, Lexend, Andika and Noto Music (all SIL Open Font License), with `fonts/fonts.css`.
- The wordmark is inline SVG outlines made from Cinzel and Caveat (both SIL Open Font License), so it needs no font request.
- `favicon.svg`, `favicon-32.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`: the arch-and-staff mark (the SVG follows the system's light or dark mode).
- `gcs-accidentals.woff2`: two zero-advance glyphs (♯ ♭) derived from Noto Music so "C♭" is as wide as "C"; also embedded in the pages.
