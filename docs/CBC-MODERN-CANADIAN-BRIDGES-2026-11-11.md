# CBC Modern Canadian Bridges — pilot plan

Date: 11 November 2026

Branch: `claude/tune-workshop-editing-7e81a1`

Status: ten new originals at `classroom-check`; no public catalogue or deployment change

## Purpose

This pilot adds one distinct contemporary Canadian listening lens for every Ontario grade band from JK through Grade 8. The source recordings stay outside the repository and remain copyrighted. Each workshop record contains a new CC0 classroom composition, an authorized listening link, an explicit distance statement, two live improvisation/arranging decisions, all nine dossier fields, a bar-by-bar reading note and named human reviews.

The central improvisation model is **choice plus conversational timing**: a player receives a small number of audible options, acts inside a known time window, and another player or group responds. At later grades the menu opens from timbre and dynamics to pitch variation, flow, texture, production drops and ensemble arrangement. These are useful pillars, though not the whole of improvisation: shared vocabulary, attention, memory, risk, revision and consent also matter.

## Copyright and cultural boundary

- “Inspired by” means a question for listening: space, hook, articulation, place-writing, phrasing, colour, pocket, flow, density or collective texture.
- It does **not** mean copying a melody, lyric, flow, chord sequence, sample, production sound, signature instrumental figure or cultural material.
- Every source is named and linked; every new score is identified as AI-assisted, original and CC0.
- `Signal Drop` deliberately excludes powwow drum patterns, vocables, chants and samples. Its subject is electronic arrangement and collaboration, and it remains blocked on Indigenous review.
- `Screenlight` remains blocked on Francophone language and cultural review. `Blue Room Answer` and `Room for the Verse` require Black Canadian genre specialists.

## Review sequence

1. Second score reader verifies metre, pitch, underlay, range and originality distance.
2. Grade-band teacher runs a small classroom trial and records tempo, range, accessibility and cue clarity.
3. Named cultural/genre reviewers check context, terminology and the limits of the bridge.
4. Student listening panels compare the source and original, identify what is shared at the element level, and flag anything that feels imitative or inauthentic.
5. Only after those checks should a record advance from `classroom-check` or be considered for the main catalogue.

## CBC research note

CBC Listen describes hundreds of curated playlists and searchable genres; CBC regulatory reporting identifies all-Canadian, Indigenous, emerging and French-language discoverability as part of the service. CBC pages were not directly readable by the research environment, so current chart membership was checked through the indexed CBC Music Top 20 mirror, while artist/label pages and CBC Music's verified YouTube channel supplied the stable listening links. The records state this provenance rather than presenting the mirror as the copyright owner.
