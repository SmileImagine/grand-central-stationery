// Traditional rhythms from beyond the drum kit, typed for the course from how
// they are taught in schools and community classes. They are folk patterns,
// nobody's composition, and several are simplified teaching versions (said so
// in the note). Instrument names stand in: low is a bass drum or the dum of a
// goblet drum, rim or tom its tak, bell a gankogui or kenong, wood a kethuk.

// ---- South Asia ----

title: Teentaal theka (tabla, 16 matras)
origin: North India
bpm: 80
meter: 4/4
note: dha dhin dhin dha | dha dhin dhin dha | dha tin tin ta | ta dhin dhin dha. The low line is the bayan, open where the bols have dha or dhin and silent in the khali (third bar).

low:  X x x x | o x x x | x . . . | . x x x

title: Keherwa (8 matras)

low:  xx .. .. x.

title: Dadra (6 matras)

title: Rupak (7 matras, 3+2+2)
origin: North India

meter: 7/8

title: Adi tala (8 beats, clapped)
origin: South India (Carnatic)
bpm: 90
meter: 4/4
note: laghu of four (clap, then three finger counts), then two drutams (clap, wave). Say ta ka di mi ta ka ju nu over it; the Syllables view's konnakol setting speaks the subdivisions.
clap: X o o o | x . x .
pat:  . . . . | . x . x

title: Konnakol: ta ka di mi, ta ki ta, ta ka
origin: South India (Carnatic)
bpm: 90
meter: 4/4
note: a 4+3+2 phrase fitted to three beats and rests to the fourth; each group speaks its own syllables.
clap: x . . . | x . . . | x . . . | x . . .
wood: xxxx xxx. xx.. ....

meter: 12/8
note: the twelve-pulse bell (gankogui) over a drum on each of the four main beats.

low:  x..x..x..x..

title: Standard bell with the six beats
