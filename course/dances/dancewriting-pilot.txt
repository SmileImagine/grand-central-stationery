title: Orientale, first phrase
origin: Isadora Duncan, Orientale (a Chopin dance, to the Mazurka in A minor, Op. 68 No. 2), reconstructed by Hortense Kooluris
source: Orientale as taught by Sylvia Gold, written in Sutton DanceWriting by Alison Kincaid (The Center for Sutton Movement Writing, 2000), page 4, lines 1 and 2: https://www.dancewriting.org/library/duncan/index.html
note: read for the rig, not transcribed symbol for symbol. A bar's wait, then three bars of light travelling steps, one on every count, the hands lifted near the shoulders with the wrists bent back (lower and wider in the second bar), and a bar of three striking steps in place, the arms down. The notation turns the shoulders and hips a little to the diagonal; this keeps the dancer square to the front.
meter: 3/4
bpm: 132
feet:  .  .  .  | Lf^ Rf^ Lf^ | Rf^ Lf^ Rf^ | Lf^ Rf^ Lf^ | Rp!_ Lp!_ Rp!_

pose wide: arms=70,40 hand=flat lean=8 tilt=-8 look=-15
pose down: arms=12,25 hand=flat nod=15 stretch=.95
