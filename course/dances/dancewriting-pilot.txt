title: Orientale, first phrase
origin: Isadora Duncan, Orientale (a Chopin dance, to the Mazurka in A minor, Op. 68 No. 2), reconstructed by Hortense Kooluris
source: Orientale as taught by Sylvia Gold, written in Sutton DanceWriting by Alison Kincaid (The Center for Sutton Movement Writing, 2000), page 4, lines 1 and 2: https://www.dancewriting.org/library/duncan/index.html
note: read for the rig, not transcribed symbol for symbol. A bar's wait, then three bars of a lilting Duncan run, one step on every count, the hands lifted near the shoulders with the wrists bent back (lower and wider in the second bar): on 1 a step with the free knee lifting forward, on 2 a step into a bent knee with the other leg trailing behind, on 3 the feet gathering and rising onto the balls; the second bar mirrors which leg leads the gesture. Then a bar of three striking steps in place, the arms down. The facing signs under the staff turn the shoulders and hips to a diagonal, one way in the first and third bars and the other in the second (read here as the dancer's right diagonal first; swap r and l in the turn: line if the key says otherwise); the striking steps face the front.
