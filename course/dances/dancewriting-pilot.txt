title: Orientale, first phrase
origin: Isadora Duncan, Orientale (a Chopin dance, to the Mazurka in A minor, Op. 68 No. 2), reconstructed by Hortense Kooluris
source: Orientale as taught by Sylvia Gold, written in Sutton DanceWriting by Alison Kincaid (The Center for Sutton Movement Writing, 2000), page 4, lines 1 and 2: https://www.dancewriting.org/library/duncan/index.html
note: read for the rig with the key in DanceWriting for Modern & Jazz Dance (Sutton, 1979), not transcribed symbol for symbol. A bar's wait, then three bars of a lilting Duncan run, one step on every count, the hands lifted near the shoulders with the wrists bent back (lower and wider in the second bar): on 1 a step with the free knee lifting forward, on 2 a step into a bent knee with the other leg trailing behind, on 3 the feet gathering; the second bar mirrors which leg leads. The light and dark circles at the floor are the weight signs (light the left foot, dark the right), which is where the feet: line comes from; the darkened feet mark the limb nearest the corner faced, not a rise. Then a bar of three striking steps in place, the arms down. The position symbols under the staff (an overhead view, the audience at the bottom, the centre line the body's front-to-back line) turn the body to the dancer's right front corner in the first and third bars and the left front corner in the second, with the limbs projecting along that diagonal; the striking steps face the front. Their numbers are rotation numbers: the arms partly turned in (3), the legs parallel (2), which a parallel knee lifting toward a corner shows from the front as a knee opening to the side. The arms' rotation (which way the palms face) is not drawn.
