**Subject:** Permission request: Labanotation motifs for a free classroom dance tool

Dear colleagues,

I am an elementary music teacher in [city, province], and I maintain Grand Central Stationery, a free, open-source classroom display for music and movement (https://smileimagine.github.io/grand-central-stationery/, source under the AGPL-3.0 at https://github.com/SmileImagine/grand-central-stationery). It plays rhythms and dance steps typed as short text and draws them as footprints, as a Labanotation-style column score, and as an animated figure, so that a class can learn a step sequence alongside a video of the dance.

The tool grades steps on a six-level ladder for a free, graded course. Its dance material is thin and almost entirely Western European and North American, because that is what exists in the public domain. The motif collection of your Archive (the Labanotation motifs of Hungarian traditional dances published through the Néptánc Tudástár, and the film clips they accompany) is the richest step-level record of a folk tradition I know of, and I am writing to ask whether some of it might be used in this project.

Specifically, I would like to ask permission to:

1. transcribe a selection of basic motifs (for example [a few named motif types or dance types, e.g. the ugrós, csárdás and verbunk basics]) into the tool's step language, so that students can see and practise them, with each motif credited to the Archive, to its notator, and to the dance and village of origin;
2. link to the Archive's own pages for the motif and its film clip, so that students go to your material rather than a copy;
3. state the terms you set, in the tool's sources file, so that any reuse downstream is governed by them.

I am not asking for copies of films or notation files to be redistributed, nor for commercial use of any kind. If the Archive prefers, the transcriptions can be kept out of the open-source repository and published only on the project's site under a separate notice, or restricted to the specific motifs you approve.

I would also welcome advice on how the Archive would like the material cited, and whether a member of your staff should review the transcriptions before they are shown to students.

Thank you for the work of the Archive and for considering this request.
