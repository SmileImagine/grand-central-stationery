# Sansa Akroma human-review packet

Status: **packet prepared 11 November 2026; no reviewer has been contacted and no human review has occurred.** This packet is a request for evidence, not a sign-off.

## What is being reviewed

- Workshop record: [`../items/sansa-akroma.abc`](../items/sansa-akroma.abc)
- Source: Akosua Obuo Addo and Justina Adu, "Examining the Use of Folk Resources for Creative Arts Education in Ghana's Basic Schools," *International Journal of Education & the Arts* 23(4), 2022, Figure 3 p. 18 and interview pp. 17-18, [doi:10.26209/ijea23n4](https://doi.org/10.26209/ijea23n4).
- Reuse basis: the article is CC BY-NC 4.0. The workshop ABC is a noncommercial transcription carrying the same attribution and licence; it is not cleared for the public AGPL repertoire.
- Current evidence: the article describes an interviewed Ghanaian early-years teacher moving stones, or oranges in her class, one child at a time while the children sing. The teacher uses the game for counting, vocabulary, reasoning and eye-hand coordination. Figure 3 supplies an eight-bar 2/4 melody and the interview supplies words.
- Known limits: the article does not name the language, community, tempo, exact passing direction/timing, translation, instrumental practice or a recording. The printed first-ending bracket has no visible second ending.

## Who is needed

The review needs all three roles; one person may fill more than one only when they can state the relevant competence.

1. A Ghanaian early-years teacher who knows this specific game in practice.
2. A competent speaker of the language used in the printed words, once the language is identified.
3. A classroom music teacher who can test the range, tempo, passing action and accessibility with children.

Sensible first contacts, **not reviewers of record and not yet contacted**, are the article's authors:

- Akosua Obuo Addo, University of Minnesota music education, whose official profile lists Ghanaian children's play, elementary methods and African music: <https://cla.umn.edu/about/directory/profile/addox002>.

Authorship or institutional affiliation does not by itself establish competence in the unidentified source language. Either author could instead nominate the interviewed teacher or another appropriate speaker/practitioner.

## Questions for the Ghanaian practitioner and language reviewer

Please answer only from your own knowledge and say when a question needs someone else.

1. Do you know this specific game? Where, when and from whom did you learn or observe it?
2. What language and local variety are the printed words in? Do they identify a particular community, or should the record remain simply "Ghanaian"?
3. Are these words spelled and divided correctly? Please supply a checked line, tone/diacritic corrections if needed, a literal translation and a child-friendly translation.
4. How should each word be pronounced? A slow spoken demonstration and one sung demonstration would be ideal, but only if the reviewer consents to the project retaining or linking it.
5. Does Figure 3 match the melody you know? Please list corrections by bar number. How is the unsupported first-ending bracket normally handled?
6. What is the usual pulse or approximate tempo? Is the current quarter note = 96 too slow, too fast or reasonable for rehearsal?
7. Exactly how are stones, oranges or another object moved: direction, one object or several, one pass per beat/bar/phrase, and what happens at the repeat or ending?
8. Who normally leads and who responds? Is the game sung in unison, call-and-response or another form?
9. Are handclaps, body percussion, instruments or harmony normally present? If the answer is no or varies, please say so; the project will not import a Ghanaian bell/drum pattern from geography alone.
10. Is the game secular and suitable for a mixed classroom? Are there age, gender, setting, ownership, sacred/ceremonial or other respect limits?
11. Is "counting and passing game" an honest classroom function, and is C4-A4 in C a practical rehearsal key for young voices?
12. May the project credit the reviewer by name, role and community relationship? May it quote the response? State any anonymity or reuse conditions.

## Questions for the classroom music teacher

1. Age/grade and number of students tested; prior familiarity, if any.
2. Whether C4-A4 in C was comfortable, and any safer starting pitch.
3. A workable rehearsal sequence that preserves the reviewed game action.
4. Whether quarter note = 96 supported accurate words and passing.
5. Any access or safety changes needed for students who cannot pass an object hand-to-hand.
6. Whether the tune should remain in the workshop, be revised and retested, or be rejected for this course.
